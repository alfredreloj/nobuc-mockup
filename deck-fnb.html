Why it matters

Five places the margin leaks — all of them invisible without one system.

₱90,000 / month

Food cost drift

Menu prices are set once a year; ingredient costs move weekly. Two points of drift on ₱4.5M monthly sales is ₱90,000 — and nobody sees it until the P&L closes.

₱45,000 / month

Wastage off the books

Spoilage and staff meals written on a notebook never reach the ledger. One percent of sales unlogged quietly becomes "shrinkage" at year-end count.

Untracked

Commissary yield

The central kitchen issues 100 kg of raw and books 100 kg of finished. Real yield, trim loss and rework never get journalised, so branch food cost absorbs the gap.

Payroll and service charge

Night differential, holiday premiums and RA 11360 service-charge distribution computed in a spreadsheet is exactly where a DOLE finding starts — and where staff trust ends.

Tax exposure

When POS sales, book sales and the VAT return disagree, reconstructing the tie-out during an audit costs more in time and professional fees than the assessment itself.

Module · Recipes & food cost

Know what every plate costs — today, not next quarter.

  • A recipe for every menu item, down to sub-recipes — sauces, doughs, pre-mixes, portioned proteins.
  • Every ring at the till relieves ingredients at moving-average cost. That is your theoretical usage, calculated automatically.
  • Physical count posts actual usage. The difference is variance — per branch, per item, per day, with a reason code.
  • Menu engineering, live: contribution margin per item, ranked, so you know what to push and what to re-price.
  • Simulate a price move: "if pork belly rises ₱30/kg, here is the new margin on every item that uses it."

Module · Commissary & production

The central kitchen becomes a costed, auditable step — not a black box.

  • Production order from a recipe: issue the raw materials, receive the finished goods, book the yield variance in the same document.
  • Wastage, trim and rework captured where they happen, with a reason code, at cost.
  • Transfer to branch at cost — one document posts both sides. No branch re-encodes a delivery receipt.
  • Every run leaves a journal entry. Your accountant sees work-in-process and finished goods, not an unexplained inventory jump.
  • Standard vs actual per run, so a bad batch is visible the day it happens.

Module · Philippine payroll

Restaurant payroll is the hard kind. It's built in, not bolted on.

  • SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and withholding tax on the current tables, per employee, with the remittance schedules.
  • Shift reality: night differential, rest days, regular and special holidays, overtime tiers, late and undertime — read from biometrics, not retyped.
  • Service charge under RA 11360: pooled, distributed 100% to covered employees at least twice a month, with a per-employee sheet you can show anyone who asks.
  • 13th month, final pay, loans and cash advances tracked against the employee, not a side spreadsheet.
  • Approved payroll posts to the ledger as a journal entry — labour cost lands in the same P&L as food cost, on the same day.
